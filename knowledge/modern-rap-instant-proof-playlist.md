# Sixty-Five Songs That Detonated On Contact

Modern rap's 2012–2024 run was the first era in which a song could go from a bedroom upload to a national chant with no label, no radio and no album cycle, and that is what this playlist proves. Sixty-five tracks below clear a single test: hip-hop communities (a videographer's YouTube channel, a Vine loop, SoundCloud, WorldStar, Lyrical Lemonade, a Rolling Loud pit, rap Twitter/TikTok) lit the fuse first, and the fuse burned fast. The hardest numbers in the set are unambiguous: "Hot Nigga" went from a dormant YouTube video to **5 million views in one month** the moment one Vine user looped the Shmoney Dance ([Wikipedia: Hot Nigga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Nigga); [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/pro/how-bobby-shmurda-hot-boy-came-to-life/)); "XO Tour Llif3" did **6.64 million SoundCloud plays in two weeks** off a leak with no cover art ([Billboard](https://billboard.com/articles/columns/hip-hop/8062541/soundcloud-playback-list-2017-lil-uzi-vert-top-artists)); "Gucci Gang" pulled **75 million YouTube views in three weeks** ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/lil-pump-gucci-gang)); "Shotta Flow" turned **915,000 first-week views into a $3 million bidding war inside 30 days** ([Wikipedia: Shotta Flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shotta_Flow); [Billboard](https://billboard.com/articles/business/8498052/memphis-rapper-nle-choppa-label-bidding-war)); and "Not Like Us" set the all-time rap single-day Spotify record with **~12.8 million streams on day four** ([HotNewHipHop](https://www.hotnewhiphop.com/800491-kendrick-lamar-not-like-us-drake-diss-record-most-streams-one-day-spotify-hip-hop-news)). 2016–17 is the peak because streaming became the measured format, RapCaviar replaced radio as the ignition switch, Rolling Loud industrialized the moshpit and Cole Bennett gave unsigned kids arena-sized visuals, all in the same 24 months. Pop crossovers ("Old Town Road," "In My Feelings," "Watch Me") and slow-burn radio climbs ("Trap Queen," "Bodak Yellow," "No Flockin," "Faneto") are cut, with the reasoning stated. One structural caveat governs every number here: all five researchers worked from search snippets only, so every figure needs a spot-check before publication.

## 1. Core truths

The era detonated because the audience took over distribution: in 2012 a Chicago videographer's YouTube channel *was* the chart, and "I Don't Like" did **30 million views on DGainz's personal upload** before Interscope wrote a **$6 million** check within six months ([Wikipedia: I Don't Like](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Don%27t_Like); [HipHopDX](https://hiphopdx.com/news/chief-keefs-deal-with-interscope-records-revealed-to-be-worth-6-million/)). By 2014 the unit of ignition was six seconds long: a single Vine of Bobby Shmurda's hat toss dragged a three-month-old video to **5M views in 30 days** and onto Beyoncé's stage by July 15 ([Slate](https://www.slate.com/blogs/browbeat/2014/07/15/shmoney_dance_bobby_shmurda_viral_hit_goes_from_vines_to_beyonce_and_jay.html)), and OG Maco's "U Guessed It" logged **9M Vine loops, 16M YouTube plays and ~7M SoundCloud plays** inside four months on a #90 chart peak ([Complex](http://www.complex.com/music/2014/12/rap-one-hit-wonder-2014-songs/og-maco-u-guessed-it)). From 2015 the platform was SoundCloud plus one superstar tweet or sample: "Panda" went from a $200 beat lease to Kanye's *Life of Pablo* to **#1 on May 7, 2016** in under five months ([Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/pro/desiigner-panda-billboard-hot-100-number-1/)), and Drake signed iLoveMakonnen **19 days** after tweeting the "Tuesday" remix ([Wikipedia: ILoveMakonnen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ILoveMakonnen)). Those are the waves in order: Chicago drill (2012–14), the NYC/Vine street era (2013–16), the Atlanta co-sign-and-meme machine (2013–17), SoundCloud rap (2015–19), the 2015–18 event-drop and moshpit peak, the Lyrical Lemonade/WorldStar YouTube era (2017–19), the regional street wave from Detroit, Memphis, LA and Brooklyn (2016–21), and the TikTok 2020s.

2016–17 was the peak because four independent curves crossed at once. Hip-hop became the most-streamed US genre in 2017 inside a market of **618 billion on-demand streams**, so for the first time rap's audience and the industry's measuring stick were the same thing ([Tippie/Univ. of Iowa](https://tippie.uiowa.edu/news/2023/08/tuma-basa-sets-hip-hop-trends)). Spotify's RapCaviar, curated by Tuma Basa, grew by **more than 3 million followers in a year** to roughly **8.8–9 million listeners** and functioned as a career detonator rather than a mirror: Migos and Lil Uzi Vert "became multiplatinum stars after receiving Basa's endorsement" ([Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/articles/business/8225270/tuma-basa-curator-spotify-rapcaviar-playlist-heading-youtube); [Iowa Now](https://now.uiowa.edu/news/2018/02/ui-alumnus-tuma-basa-setting-trends-hip-hop)), and "Bodak Yellow" shows the switch in action, added to RapCaviar **four days after release** with a **+124% first-week streaming jump** ([Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/articles/columns/hip-hop/7968874/how-cardi-b-bodak-yellow-exploded-streaming-services)). Rolling Loud, founded in Miami in 2015, moved to Bayfront Park in 2017 and cloned itself into Bay Area and SoCal editions the same year, selling out at roughly **40,000 a night** before growing to **180,000+ in 2018** and **200,000–210,000 in 2019** ([Miami New Times](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/music/rolling-loud-2017-tickets-sold-out-9305649/); [Business Today](https://journal.businesstoday.org/bt-online/2020/the-marketability-of-rolling-loud)), and the defining image of the year was XXXTentacion at Rolling Loud Miami on **May 6, 2017**, "the entire crowd moshing and raging" while security pulled him from the pit ([XXL](https://www.xxlmag.com/xxxtentacion-performs-at-2017-rolling-loud-festival/)). Cole Bennett's Lyrical Lemonade was already shooting Famous Dex, Lil Pump, Smokepurpp and Ski Mask in 2016–17, went platinum with "Betrayed" in August 2017 and landed "Lucid Dreams" in May 2018, a free visual pipeline that turned SoundCloud uploads into YouTube events ([Lyrical Lemonade](https://blog.lyricallemonade.com/p/director/cole-bennett/)). The scale signal of the peak is SoundCloud's own year-end: "XO Tour Llif3" was its **#1 song of 2017 at 148 million streams, 5.5x the top pop song** ([The FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2017/12/08/lil-uzi-vert-xo-tour-llif3-top-song-soundcloud-2017)), and "Lucid Dreams" its **#1 of 2018 at 123 million** ([Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/pro/soundcloud-2018-juice-wrld-top-artist-track-lucid-dreams/)). The ceiling event was *DAMN.*: **50M+ streams in 24 hours, 340.6M in week one**, and "HUMBLE." at **~500,000 YouTube views in 90 minutes** ([Revolt](https://www.revolt.tv/article/2017-04-22/26905/kendrick-lamar-has-the-years-best-first-week-sales-and-streams-with-damn); [WESA](https://www.wesanews.org/2017-03-30/kendrick-lamars-video-for-humble-is-a-must-watch)). Two honesty notes govern the ranking below: raw day-one stream counts are not comparable across eras (Spotify did not even publish single-day song records in 2017, and *DAMN.*'s 50M-in-24-hours is an album total while "Not Like Us"'s 10.986M is one song on one service, seven years of platform growth apart), so the cross-era signal is whether a crowd chanted it within weeks and whether the drop generated argument; and Vine's shutdown erased the loop counts, so 2013–16 entries are scored on proxies such as signing lag, remix lag and chart-debut position. Chart peak is anti-correlated with purity of ignition in this set: "Versace" peaked at **#99** while rewiring the genre's flow ([Wikipedia: Versace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versace_(song))), and "Old Town Road" spent 19 weeks at #1 and was never a rap-community record.

## 3. The 10-Song "Instant Proof" Pack

These are the ten with the hardest, least arguable ignition evidence, chosen so that a skeptic hears the mechanism change every three songs: a videographer's channel, a Vine loop, a Kanye sample, a SoundCloud leak, a WorldStar/Lemonade video, a diss track.

1. **Hot Nigga — Bobby Shmurda (2014).** The cleanest platform detonation in the era: a video that did nothing for three months, then **5M YouTube views in one month** the moment one Vine user looped the hat toss ([Wikipedia: Hot Nigga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Nigga)). The Shmoney Dance was on Beyoncé's stage by July 15 and Epic paid a rumored $2M for an unsigned 20-year-old ([Slate](https://www.slate.com/blogs/browbeat/2014/07/15/shmoney_dance_bobby_shmurda_viral_hit_goes_from_vines_to_beyonce_and_jay.html); [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/pro/bobby-shmurda-epic-records-label-released/)).
2. **I Don't Like — Chief Keef ft. Lil Reese (2012).** A 16-year-old on house arrest turned a videographer's YouTube channel into a national A&R feed: **30M+ views on DGainz's upload**, a Kanye remix **seven weeks** after the video, a **$6M** Interscope deal within six months ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Don%27t_Like); [Stereogum](https://stereogum.com/1019512/kanye-west-i-dont-like-remix-feat-chief-keef-pusha-t-jadakiss-big-sean/news); [HipHopDX](https://hiphopdx.com/news/chief-keefs-deal-with-interscope-records-revealed-to-be-worth-6-million/)). Every later wave on this list is downstream of this video.
3. **Panda — Desiigner (2015/16).** A $200 beat lease by a nobody went from SoundCloud (Dec 15, 2015) into Kanye's album (Feb 14, 2016) to **#1 in America on May 7, 2016**, with **22.3M weekly US streams (+48%)** and the Streaming Gainer on the way up ([Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/pro/desiigner-panda-hot-rap-songs-chart/); [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/pro/desiigner-panda-billboard-hot-100-number-1/)). The crossover was the consequence, not the cause.
4. **XO Tour Llif3 — Lil Uzi Vert (2017).** A leak with no cover art, no video and no label push did **6.64M SoundCloud plays in 14 days** and finished as SoundCloud's #1 song of 2017 at **148M streams, 5.5x the top pop song** ([Billboard](https://billboard.com/articles/columns/hip-hop/8062541/soundcloud-playback-list-2017-lil-uzi-vert-top-artists); [The FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2017/12/08/lil-uzi-vert-xo-tour-llif3-top-song-soundcloud-2017)). "All my friends are dead" became the generational chant and the song went Diamond.
5. **Magnolia — Playboi Carti (2017).** The benchmark that runs on cultural evidence rather than a counter: the fans picked it off the mixtape, Complex had it in the Song of the Summer race within a month, Jay-Z called it "incredible," and Carti did Kimmel in June ([Complex](http://www.complex.com/music/2017/05/contenders-for-song-of-the-summer-2017/playboi-carti-magnolia); [Complex](https://www.complex.com/music/2017/06/playboi-carti-performance-magnolia-jimmy-kimmel-live)). No first-week number was recovered; it stays in the pack on consensus speed.
6. **Gucci Gang — Lil Pump (2017).** The largest 21-day YouTube number in the research: **75M+ views in three weeks** off the Oct 23, 2017 video, **108M+ combined streams** by late October, and an **$8M** Warner deal months later ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/lil-pump-gucci-gang); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gucci_Gang); [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/music/features/juice-wrld-billboard-cover-story-2019-8501291/)). Top 40 arrived late; the first wave was SoundCloud and memes.
7. **Lucid Dreams — Juice WRLD (2017/18).** A Chicago-suburb teenager who had played one show turned a SoundCloud loosie (June 15, 2017) into a **$3M Interscope deal** nine months later, then Cole Bennett's video (May 11, 2018) became the Lyrical Lemonade era's defining artifact ([Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/music/features/juice-wrld-billboard-cover-story-2019-8501291/)). SoundCloud's own 2018 year-end crowned it **#1 at 123M plays**, the decisive community-first proof ([Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/pro/soundcloud-2018-juice-wrld-top-artist-track-lucid-dreams/)).
8. **Shotta Flow — NLE Choppa (2019).** A 16-year-old's phone-shot yard video did **915,000 views in week one and 10M in a month**, triggering a **$3M** three-label bidding war inside 30 days, and he said no ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shotta_Flow); [Billboard](https://billboard.com/articles/business/8498052/memphis-rapper-nle-choppa-label-bidding-war)). The cleanest week-1-to-month-1 curve in the whole set.
9. **Ransom — Lil Tecca (2019).** One Cole Bennett video (May 22, 2019) did **21M+ views in under a month** and put an unsigned 16-year-old on the Hot 100 at **#93 three weeks after upload**, then #4 in about eight weeks while adding **3.3M Spotify streams a day** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ransom_(Lil_Tecca_song)); [Billboard](https://assets.billboard.com/articles/columns/hip-hop/8546305/lil-tecca-ransom-breakout-artist-2019-interview)). Radio followed; it did not lead.
10. **Not Like Us — Kendrick Lamar (2024).** The one 2020s record that clears the 2016 bar on every axis at once: **10.986M Spotify streams on day one, ~12.8M on day four** (the all-time rap single-day record), a video that did **18–20M views in under 24 hours**, and a city chanting it five or six times in a row at The Pop Out on June 19 ([Vibe](https://www.vibe.com/music/music-news/kendrick-lamar-not-like-us-breaks-spotify-streams-record-drake-1234876810/); [HotNewHipHop](https://www.hotnewhiphop.com/800491-kendrick-lamar-not-like-us-drake-diss-record-most-streams-one-day-spotify-hip-hop-news); [Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2024/07/05/kendrick-lamar-not-like-us-music-video/); [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-live-reviews/kendrick-lamars-juneteenth-concert-the-pop-out-1235042890/)).

**Recommended play order:** Hot Nigga → I Don't Like → Panda → XO Tour Llif3 → Magnolia → Gucci Gang → Lucid Dreams → Shotta Flow → Ransom → Not Like Us. Cold-open on the most physical record so the skeptic moves before they think, then run the remaining nine chronologically so they hear the platform change under the music (director's channel → Kanye sample → SoundCloud leak → Lemonade video → diss track), alternating hard and melodic so no three songs share an energy, and close on the only 2020s record big enough to send them back to the start.

## 4. Honorable mentions / cut list

**Rule applied to delayed detonations.** A song whose explosion came months after upload stays in the master list only if the explosion itself was a community-first event (a Vine, a crowd-video wave, a rap-Twitter meme, a co-sign) rather than a radio or playlist build. Under that rule "Mo Bamba," "Bad and Boujee," "Look at Me!," "Ultimate," "One Night" and "Thotiana" stay in; "Faneto," "No Flockin," "Trap Queen," "Bodak Yellow" and "Dior" go out.

**Contested calls the researchers flagged, decided:**

- **Faneto (Chief Keef, 2014): CUT.** The Chicago Reader wrote a whole piece on it as a "slow-growing underground hit," Complex calls it a slow build with a 2018 resurgence, and the only dated event is a Clemson floor collapsing in 2018 ([Chicago Reader](https://chicagoreader.com/blogs/the-unexpected-appeal-of-faneto-chief-keefs-slow-growing-underground-hit/); [Complex](https://www.complex.com/music/a/kiana/chief-keef-on-legacy-of-faneto)). There is no datable detonation event, so it fails the delayed-detonation rule despite being the decade's most-cited pit anthem. Researchers 1 and 5 both recommended cutting it; they win.
- **Mo Bamba (Sheck Wes, 2017/18): KEEP.** A ~15-month fuse, but the explosion was crowd video and word of mouth "without record label or radio support," then #82 to #6 in eight weeks ([Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/pro/sheck-wes-mo-bamba-hot-100-soundcloud/)). Community-first explosion, so it survives.
- **Bad and Boujee (Migos, 2016): KEEP.** The FADER's 100-tweets-on-release-day to ~600,000-posts curve is the one quantitative portrait of a rap meme detonating, and the trigger was Twitter plus a Golden Globes speech, not radio ([The FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2017/01/04/migos-bad-and-boujee-raindrop-meme)). Researcher 5's "3.5 day-one" is correct, and the rule above resolves it in favor of inclusion.
- **Trap Queen (Fetty Wap, 2014): CUT.** SoundCloud-first, yes ("six figures within weeks" with no radio or blogs), but the ignition took five months to reach Hot 97 and the explosion from there was radio-carried to #2 ([Complex](https://www.complex.com/music/a/lauren-nostro/the-making-of-fetty-wap-trap-queen)). Slow burn, radio build. Researcher 1's score of 3 beats Researcher 5's unsourced "lean keep."
- **Old Town Road (Lil Nas X, 2019): CUT.** The highest-velocity object in the dataset and the least hip-hop-native: a #yeehaw TikTok trend the artist seeded himself, then a country-chart controversy ([Capital FM](https://www.capitalfm.com/internet/i-got-the-horses-in-the-back-memes/)). Pop crossover and engineered virality; it defines the exclusion rule.
- **In My Feelings (Drake, 2018): CUT.** Shiggy's Instagram clip was community-first and drew 450,000+ challenge videos, but the song was an album cut by the most radio-saturated artist alive; Shiggy went famous overnight, Drake did not ([Big Top 40](https://www.bigtop40.com/features/drake-shiggy-in-my-feelings-challenge/)). Pop crossover.
- **Bodak Yellow (Cardi B, 2017): CUT, narrowly.** Club-first and built on "No Flockin," so not a radio record, but its curve is a documented three-month compounding climb (+124%, +31%, +14%, +30%) from a playlist add to #1 on Sept 14 ([Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/articles/columns/hip-hop/7968874/how-cardi-b-bodak-yellow-exploded-streaming-services)). A build, not a bang. It is the best case study for the Core truths section and the closest call in this list.
- **The Box (Roddy Ricch, 2019/20): KEEP.** Researcher 5 leaned cut (general-TikTok meme); Researchers 3 and 4 keep. Keep wins because it was never a single, had no launch video, and radio followed streaming by weeks: an album cut the audience promoted past Bieber's "Yummy" to #1 ([Slate](https://slate.com/culture/2020/01/roddy-ricch-the-box-hot-100-tiktok-justin-bieber.html)).
- **SICKO MODE (Travis Scott, 2018): KEEP.** Superstar rollout, but the first wave was entirely rap-community (64.5M day-one Apple streams, 55.1M song streams in week one) and the Hot 100 lagged the detonation by 17 weeks ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/apple-music-beats-spotify-to-biggest-day-one-streaming-number-for-travis-scotts-astroworld/); [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/pro/travis-scott-first-streaming-songs-number-1-lead-artist-sicko-mode/)).
- **HUMBLE. (Kendrick Lamar, 2017): KEEP.** A surprise video that did ~500K views in 90 minutes and was rap Twitter's before it was anyone's; same mechanism class as "Like That." The unverified Spotify day-one record is not used.
- **Dior (Pop Smoke, 2019): CUT.** Its national moment (#22, ~7 months after release) came posthumously and through protest summer, not from the drop ([Complex](https://www.complex.com/music/brooklyn-drill-the-new-sound-of-new-york/)). "Welcome to the Party" carries the Pop Smoke detonation instead.
- **Lucid Dreams (Juice WRLD): DEFENDED, KEEP.** SoundCloud had it for 11 months before radio; 2M+ plays got the deal; SoundCloud's own year-end crowned it. Community-first beyond argument.
- **No Flockin (Kodak Black, 2014): CUT.** DJBooth's contemporaneous headline settles it: "a Hit Almost Two Years After It Was Released" (Apr 13, 2016); 20M plays took 20 months ([DJBooth](https://djbooth.net/features/2016-04-13-kodak-black-no-flockin-hit/)). A second Faneto.
- **Whole Lotta Choppas (Sada Baby, 2020): KEEP, flagged.** The mechanism was a creator-originated TikTok challenge (@ohbukster) that reached Heidi Klum, which is the same mechanism as "Savage" (Keara Wilson) and is treated the same way; the label acted after the virality (Nicki remix Oct 16) and the debut was only #92 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whole_Lotta_Choppas)).
- **FEFE (6ix9ine, 2018): CUT.** The biggest weekly stream number in the SoundCloud file (24.8M on-demand) was manufactured by a purchased Nicki feature and a retroactive add to *Queen*'s tracklist ([Complex](https://complex.com/music/2018/08/nicki-minaj-adds-6ix9ine-collab-fefe-to-queen)). Engineered virality.
- **SAD! (XXXTentacion, 2018): CUT.** Day one was unremarkable; the 10.4M single-day Spotify record came June 19, 2018, the day after his murder ([Variety](https://variety.com/2018/digital/news/xxxtentacion-breaks-taylor-swifts-single-day-global-streaming-record-on-spotify-1202852724/)). Death spike, not ignition.
- **Laugh Now Cry Later (Drake ft. Lil Durk, 2020): CUT.** Superstar single with an NFL-facility video spectacle and radio from day one; both researchers who scored it said pop crossover (unsourced verdict, editorial).

**Near misses (one line each):**

## 5. Low-confidence flags

Every number in this report was transcribed from a search-result snippet, never from the page itself (see Method note), so the flags below are the ones where the researchers found an actual conflict, an absent date or a source they distrusted.

- **"Not Like Us" peak day: 12.2M vs 12.809M, and which record it broke.** KTT2 reports 12.2M streams on day four; HotNewHipHop and ClutchPoints report 12.809M ([KTT2](https://www.ktt2.com/not-like-us-received-122-million-streams-in-its-4th-day-on-32570874); [HotNewHipHop](https://www.hotnewhiphop.com/800491-kendrick-lamar-not-like-us-drake-diss-record-most-streams-one-day-spotify-hip-hop-news); [ClutchPoints](https://clutchpoints.com/kendrick-lamars-not-like-us-breaks-massive-spotify-record-held-by-drake)). Two outlets beat one forum post, so 12.809M is used with the tilde. The record broken was Drake & Lil Baby's "Girls Want Girls" at 12.385M (2021); some snippets render it "Girls Like Girls," which is a mis-transcription, and Vibe separately cites a prior 2024 hip-hop daily high of 6.593M, a different (year-scoped) record from the all-time one. The day-one 10.986M is consistent across Vibe, Hypebeast and XXL. Pop Out performance count is five (Rolling Stone, Audacy) or six (NBC News); unresolved.
- **"Gummo" dates.** meme.com gives a YouTube upload of Oct 8, 2017 with 648,029 first-week views; the Wikipedia-derived snippet gives a Sept 24, 2017 digital release and a Nov 10, 2017 video. These cannot all be right, and the 648,029 figure comes from a low-confidence source (the underlying Penny Fractions analysis was blocked). The report uses "2017" and the first-week number with its caveat; do not publish a single premiere date without resolving this.
- **Rowdy Rebel "Computers" dates.** FADER and Rap Radar covered a video on Aug 11, 2014; Wikipedia and HotNewHipHop date the official video Oct 24, 2014. Researcher 1's second pass resolved this as "first video Aug 2014, official video Oct 24, 2014," which is plausible but unconfirmed. The song is in the cut list, so nothing rides on it.
- **"Love Sosa" ~20M.** A late-2012 source cited "over 20 million views," but the publication date could not be pinned, so the implied ~20M-in-two-months rate is an inference, marked (proxy) in the table. The 383M cumulative figure is current and safe.
- **"HUMBLE." single-day claim.** The widely repeated "Spotify US single-day record, April 2017" could not be found in any contemporaneous source after two targeted searches; only the 90-minute YouTube figure and the 49.8M weekly figure are used. Negative finding confirmed: "HUMBLE." did not hold the Vevo 24-hour record (Taylor Swift, 43.2M, Aug 2017) ([Guinness](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/112534-most-viewed-vevo-video-in-24-hours)).
- **"Thotiana" 31M.** The Wikipedia-derived sentence "gained over 31 million views despite only being released in December 2018" is internally confused (the WSHH video went up July 5, 2018). The figure is not used; the 11M-in-a-few-weeks figure for the Cole Bennett remix video is used instead. The #8 Hot 100 peak is from general knowledge, not a retrieved source.
- **"Panda" SoundCloud plays.** Researcher 1 could not recover any 30/90-day SoundCloud count; Researcher 5 found Stereogum's "more than a million plays" and its "lightly viral" framing. The report uses "1M+ plays, lightly viral" and does not claim an overnight SoundCloud explosion; the detonation was Kanye.
- **TLOP 250M Tidal streams.** Label-claimed via CNN Money and Complex, publicly disputed by DJBooth as implausible given Tidal's subscriber base ([DJBooth](https://djbooth.net/features/2016-03-29-kanye-west-the-life-of-pablo-tidal-streaming/)). Treated as unaudited and marked "disputed" in the table.
- **"Buss It" metric mismatch.** 1.2M TikTok videos (Complex, mid-Jan 2021) vs 600,000+ sound uses (TikTok's own count, Jan 11) vs 441M views. Different metrics measured days apart; they are not merged and the song is in the cut list.
- **"The Box" weekly figures.** 32M US streams "in its first week" (Slate) and 68.2M in the week ending Jan 9, 2020 (Billboard) refer to different weeks (the album week in Dec 2019 vs the climb week in January); both are used, Billboard's dated figure wins for the specific week, with a 77.2M peak week of Jan 25.
- **"Suge" 185M vs 1 billion.** "185M+ views by late 2019" and "more than a billion YouTube views in under a year" cannot both hold; HotNewHipHop separately reports 1B streams, so the 1B figure is almost certainly streams and is not used.
- **"The Race" release date and platform.** Researcher 2 has a June 16, 2017 SoundCloud upload; Researcher 5 has a June 30, 2017 YouTube drop and same-day arrest. Both could be true (audio then video); the table says "June 2017."
- **"Bad and Boujee" dates.** SoundCloud Aug 27, 2016 (HotNewHipHop timeline) vs single Oct 27/28, 2016 (FADER, Wikipedia); the report uses the SoundCloud date as the origin and the Oct 28 single as the label release.
- **"Kill Shit" ~10M.** The breakout-window figure from an AllMusic bio could not be re-confirmed on the second pass; only 50M cumulative is solid.
- **"Savage" 7.5 billion TikTok views in March 2020.** From a fandom wiki; large enough to require independent verification and used only as "most-played song of March 2020."
- **"Munch" #munch 2B views** is cumulative and undated (Neon Music); the Drake co-sign timeline is the load-bearing evidence.
- **Whole Lotta Red first-week.** 100,000 units / 112.1M US streams (Rolling Stone) vs ~160M global streams / 94,100 units (uDiscover); methodology differs, both cited.
- **Unverified Billboard peaks** reported from general knowledge by the researchers: "Suge" (#7), "Money Longer" (#92), "Thotiana" (#8), "CoCo" (top 20). None are load-bearing.
- **"Gët Busy," "family ties," "Welcome to the Party," "Big Drip," "Magnolia," "1st Day Out tha Feds," "X," "Danny Glover," "Versace":** no hard velocity number exists in any retrieved source; each is marked (proxy) in the table and scored on signing lag, remix lag, co-sign dates or crowd evidence.
- **Structural caveat.** Vine's 2017 shutdown destroyed per-loop data, and Know Your Meme (the one archive that carried dated loop counts) was blocked; the only verified Vine loop count in the entire research is OG Maco's 9M. Every 2013–16 entry other than "Hot Nigga" (5M YouTube views in a month), "Nasty Freestyle" (785,562 copies), "Ultimate" (8.6M loops on the meme Vine) and "U Guessed It" is therefore scored on proxies, and a numbers-first method systematically under-rates the Vine era relative to the TikTok era, where creation counts are published. r/hiphopheads thread metrics could not be retrieved for any song by any researcher.

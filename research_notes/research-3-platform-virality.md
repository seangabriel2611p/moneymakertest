## Key Question 1: Which platform lit the fuse, when, and what was the first-2-week velocity?

### Takeaway

Three distinct ignition mechanics dominate this lane and they are **not interchangeable**: (a) the
Vine 6-second loop (2013–2016), where a *dance* or a *quotable couplet* detached from the song and
the song rode the meme back; (b) the Instagram/Twitter challenge (2016–2019), where a named
challenge hashtag and a named originating creator (Shiggy, BlocBoy, Keara Wilson) carried it; and
(c) the TikTok sound (2019–2024), where a 7–15 second audio excerpt — often a non-lyrical *noise*
("eee-urr", the Yeat bell, the "skeeyee" ad-lib) — became a template. The hardest verifiable
velocity data exists for the TikTok era (creation counts are published by TikTok and Billboard);
**Vine loop counts are almost entirely unrecoverable** — Vine's shutdown in 2017 destroyed the
per-loop data and the archives, and no source I could reach carries dated loop counts for any 2013–16
rap Vine. This is the single largest gap in this lane.

**"Hot Nigga" — Bobby Shmurda (2014)**
- Platform + premiere channel: YouTube music video (released March 2014) → a **six-second clip cut
  from the video was re-broadcast on Vine**, which is where the "Shmoney dance" detonated. The Vine
  is the ignition, not the video. — [New Criticals](http://www.newcriticals.com/bobby-shmurda-viral-and-invisible/print)
- HARD-ish: the original clip "accumulat[ed] millions of views within a few months"; the source
  video was "now nearing 2 million views" at time of writing with "dozens of parody videos popping
  up on YouTube and Vine everyday." — [New Criticals](http://www.newcriticals.com/bobby-shmurda-viral-and-invisible/print) *(undated snapshot; treat as HARD-weak)*
- HARD (celebrity adoption, a proxy for saturation): Shmoney dance performed by Rihanna, Chris Brown,
  Drake, Taylor Swift, Justin Bieber, Megan Fox; **Beyoncé performed it during the "Flawless"
  breakdown on the On the Run tour, July 2014** — i.e. ~4 months from upload to the biggest stage in
  pop. — [Slate, 2014-07-15](https://www.slate.com/blogs/browbeat/2014/07/15/shmoney_dance_bobby_shmurda_viral_hit_goes_from_vines_to_beyonce_and_jay.html)
- VIBE: "the dance seems to have gathered momentum in part because of its versatility — you can do
  its moves to just about anything." — [Slate, 2014-07-15](https://www.slate.com/blogs/browbeat/2014/07/15/shmoney_dance_bobby_shmurda_viral_hit_goes_from_vines_to_beyonce_and_jay.html)
- Spark: a hat toss and a hip-switch, clipped to six seconds, turned a Brooklyn street record into a
  body-movement everyone on earth could perform.
- First wave: **hip-hop community, decisively.** The record was a NY street/Vine phenomenon months
  before radio; Epic signed him off the viral moment.
- Billboard footnote: No. 6 Hot 100.
- **Ignition: 5** (this is a given benchmark).
- Flags: none.

**"Tuesday" — iLoveMakonnen (2014)**
- Platform: originally "Club Goin' Up on a Tuesday," an underground/SoundCloud-era record that
  spread via **Vine and YouTube memes**; the detonation event is **Drake tweeting out a remix with a
  new verse on 2014-08-12**. Official single 2014-09-01. — [Wikipedia via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuesday_(iLoveMakonnen_song)); [Complex retrospective](https://www.complex.com/music/a/matthew-ritchie/ilovemakonnen-tuesday-drake-interview)
- HARD (signing velocity): **Makonnen signed to Drake's OVO Sound on 2014-08-31 — 19 days after the
  Drake remix tweet.** — [Wikipedia via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ILoveMakonnen)
- VIBE: "transformed from a quirky, plucky club record with some staying power into an unavoidable
  smash hit, and with the proliferation of online memes on Vine and Youtube, it made it possible to
  be trapped in a world where the least heralded day of the week felt a bit more glamorous." —
  [Complex](https://www.complex.com/music/a/matthew-ritchie/ilovemakonnen-tuesday-drake-interview)
- Spark: a co-sign tweet from the biggest rapper alive converted an internet curiosity into an
  inescapable meme about a weekday.
- First wave: **community.** Blog/Vine/SoundCloud-native before any radio.
- Billboard footnote: No. 12 Hot 100; Grammy nom, Best Rap/Sung Collaboration.
- **Ignition: 4** — enormous velocity but the fuse was a co-sign, not organic mass creation.
- Flags: none.

**"U Guessed It" — OG Maco (2014)**
- Platform: **Vine + YouTube**. Music video uploaded 2014-08-28 (dir. GOLDRUSH); single released
  2014-09-25 via Quality Control. — [Wikipedia via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U_Guessed_It)
- HARD-weak: "became a viral sensation on Vine and YouTube, garnering millions of views and
  eventually achieving platinum certification." The lyric "bitch you guessed it" became a standalone
  Vine catchphrase format. — [Know Your Meme entry, blocked from fetch](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/bitch-you-guessed-ityou-was-right); [Wikipedia via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U_Guessed_It)
- Notable: **the video preceded the single by a month** — the meme, not the release, was the product.
- Spark: a bipolar ad-lib delivery so extreme it became a reaction format before it was a song.
- First wave: **community.** Vine/Atlanta-first; radio never really carried it.
- Billboard footnote: No. 90 Hot 100 — a deliberately useful data point: **near-zero chart presence,
  maximum cultural penetration.** This is the purest "ignition over longevity" case in the lane.
- **Ignition: 4.5.**
- Flags: none. Strong candidate precisely because Billboard says nothing.

**"No Flex Zone" / "No Type" — Rae Sremmurd (2014)**

- **Ignition: 3.5** — big, but it climbed over months rather than detonating in a fortnight.
- Flags: none.

**"Flex (Ooh, Ooh, Ooh)" — Rich Homie Quan (April 2015) → "Hit the Quan" — iLoveMemphis (July 2015)**
- Platform: **Vine, then Dubsmash and Musical.ly.** The mechanic here is unusually well documented:
  the dance in Rich Homie Quan's April 2015 "Flex" video "soon became a hit with numerous Vines being
  produced portraying the dance," and **iHeartMemphis then wrote a song *about* the already-viral
  dance**, which "spawned user-uploaded videos featuring its audio on apps like Vine, Dubsmash and
  Musical.ly." — [Songfacts](https://www.songfacts.com/facts/ilovememphis/hit-the-quan); [Wikipedia via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hit_the_Quan)
- HARD: "Hit the Quan" **debuted at No. 41 on the Hot 100 dated 2015-09-05**, peaked No. 15. The
  record cost **$35 to make.** — [Songfacts](https://www.songfacts.com/facts/ilovememphis/hit-the-quan); [Wikipedia via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hit_the_Quan)
- Spark: a dance jumped hosts — from Rich Homie Quan's video to Vine to a purpose-built $35 song.
- First wave: **community** for "Flex"; "Hit the Quan" is a **platform-native product**, arguably the
  first rap record explicitly engineered *for* a pre-existing meme.
- **Ignition: "Flex" 3.5 / "Hit the Quan" 3** — the latter is derivative ignition, not primary.
- Flags: "Hit the Quan" borders on novelty/dance-craze rather than hip-hop detonation. Synthesizer
  should probably cut it and keep "Flex."

**"Watch Me (Whip/Nae Nae)" — Silentó (2015)**
- Platform: released by **Capitol Records** 2015-05-05; "almost instantly went viral, primarily on
  Twitter and Vine"; Vevo video uploaded 2015-06-25. — [Wikipedia via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watch_Me_(Whip/Nae_Nae)); [Songfacts](https://www.songfacts.com/facts/silento/watch-me-whip-nae-nae)
- HARD: music video **1.9 billion YouTube views** (undated cumulative); "quickly going over a billion

- HARD: peaked **No. 3 Hot 100 for six weeks**; **Nickelodeon promotional partnership Aug 28–Sep 6,
  2015** — a hard marker of how fast it left hip-hop and became children's/family content.
- Spark: an instructional dance song, i.e. a song whose lyrics are literally the challenge rules.
- First wave: **borderline, leaning pop.** It was Vine/Twitter-first, but it was on **Capitol from day

  Black Twitter/Vine teens, not Top 40 radio — but the second wave was so overwhelmingly
  family-pop that it arguably stopped being a hip-hop object.
- Billboard footnote: No. 3.
- **Ignition: 4** for raw velocity, but see flag.
- Flags: **LIKELY POP CROSSOVER.** My recommendation to the synthesizer: **exclude.** The Nickelodeon
  partnership within ~16 weeks is the tell — this became a school-assembly record, not a rap record.

**"Bad and Boujee" — Migos feat. Lil Uzi Vert (2016/2017)**
- Platform: **Twitter, specifically Black Twitter** — the "rain drop / drop top" snowclone. Released
  2016-10-27.
- **HARD, and the best meme-velocity dataset in this whole lane:** "There were about **100 tweets**
  quoting 'raindrop, drop top' the day the song was released." But **~82% of the total posts about
  the song on Twitter — numbering nearly 600,000 as of January 2 [2017] — came after December 7.** —
  [The FADER, 2017-01-04](https://www.thefader.com/2017/01/04/migos-bad-and-boujee-raindrop-meme) (fetch blocked; via search extraction); corroborated by [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/music/rb-hip-hop/migos-memes-donald-glover-strippers-hot-100-7662485/)
- HARD: **Donald Glover's Golden Globes shoutout (2017-01-08)** plus the meme pushed it to **No. 1
  Hot 100 for the week of 2017-01-21** — first No. 1 for both Migos and Uzi. — [The FADER, 2016-12-28](https://www.thefader.com/2016/12/28/migos-lil-uzi-vert-bad-and-boujee-meme-twitter)
- Spark: a 100-tweet-a-day record became a 600,000-post meme format in roughly four weeks, then
  a Golden Globes co-sign finished it.
- First wave: **community, textbook.** Note the ~6-week *lag* between release and meme ignition —
  this is a delayed-fuse detonation, not an instant one, which matters if the playlist thesis is
  "planet screamed it on day one."
- Billboard footnote: No. 1.
- **Ignition: 4.5** — the ceiling is extraordinary; the *ignition* was late.
- Flags: **delayed fuse** — flag for the synthesizer. Ignition ≠ release date here.

**"Mask Off" — Future (2017)**
- Platform: released 2017-02-16 on the *FUTURE* album; "became an immediate internet favorite" off

- First wave: **community.** It was an album cut that the internet promoted to a single; radio

- Billboard footnote: No. 5.
- **Ignition: 4** — enormous, but a slow-ish ~8-week ramp on an already-major artist. Not an

- Flags: none, but note it is a superstar record, not a discovery.

### Inferences

- **The velocity data is systematically asymmetric across eras.** For 2019–2024 I can cite creation
  counts, sound-use counts and a Billboard TikTok chart. For 2013–2016 I can cite essentially
  nothing platform-native — Vine's shutdown erased the loop counts, and contemporary press reported
  virality qualitatively ("millions of views," "everywhere"). **Any ignition scoring that treats
  numeric evidence as the scoring input will systematically under-rate the Vine era.** The
  synthesizer should score Vine-era entries on *proxy* evidence (celebrity adoption dates, signing
  speed, chart debut position) and say so explicitly.
- **Chart peak is anti-correlated with purity of ignition in this lane.** "U Guessed It" (No. 90) and
  "Gët Busy" (barely charted) are among the most community-native detonations; "Old Town Road"
  (19 weeks at No. 1) and "Watch Me" (No. 3, Nickelodeon) are the least. A useful heuristic for the
  synthesizer: **the more the chart loved it, the more likely the first wave was not hip-hop.**
- **The exclusion rule has a reliable structural tell: radio-playability.** "Pound Town" and "Munch"
  could not be played on terrestrial radio in their real form; "Old Town Road" and "Watch Me" were
  built for it. Where the record is structurally un-broadcastable, the first wave *must* have been
  community.
- **Three distinct co-sign archetypes recur and should not be conflated:** (1) *Drake as amplifier* —
  "Tuesday" (2014), "Look Alive" (2018), "Munch" (2022) — a 9-year-old repeating mechanic; (2) *the
  named creator* — Shiggy, Keara Wilson, BlocBoy — where a civilian, not the artist, authored the
  moment; (3) *the accidental institution* — Cam Newton, Donald Glover, college football stadiums —
  where a non-music institution detonates it.
- **The "engineered virality" line is the sharpest editorial cut available.** "Hit the Quan" (written
  about an existing meme), "Walk Em Down" (video "designed to proliferate on TikTok"), "Tap In"
  (artist-run challenge) and "Old Town Road" (artist seeding meme accounts) are all *manufactured*.
  "Savage" (creator posted five days before it caught), "Panda," "Gët Busy" and "U Guessed It" are
  not. If the playlist thesis is "detonation," the manufactured ones fail even when the numbers are
  bigger.
- **A "day one" bar would eliminate several of the biggest entries.** "Mo Bamba" took ~12 months,
  "Bad and Boujee" ~6 weeks, "Trap Queen" ~5 months (SoundCloud March 2014 → "everywhere" August
  2014 → Hot 97 → major recognition mid-November 2014). If the brief's "planet screamed it on day
  one" standard is applied literally, these are excluded; if "ignition velocity once lit" is the
  standard, they qualify. **This ambiguity needs resolving before selection.**

### Gaps

- **Vine loop counts: not recoverable, for any song, from any source I could reach.** Know Your Meme

  Machine snapshots of vine.co are the only remaining avenue and I could not attempt them within
  budget.
- **First-2-week velocity specifically** is available for essentially one song ("The Box": 32M
  streams week one). Every other number I gathered is either cumulative-to-date or measured at a
  reporting moment weeks-to-months after ignition. The brief's central metric is largely unmet by
  the public record.
- **r/hiphopheads and KTT reaction volume:** I found **zero** usable quantitative data. No source I
  reached reports thread counts, upvote counts or comment volume for any of these drops.

## Key Question 2: Did the platform moment precede radio, or follow it?

### Takeaway

For the large majority of this lane the platform preceded radio, often by months. The reliable
exceptions — the records that should be flagged as pop crossovers — are "Old Town Road," "Watch Me
(Whip/Nae Nae)," "In My Feelings," "Up," and arguably "Whats Poppin" and "Panini."

### Cited Findings

- **Platform-before-radio, hard-dated:** Kodak Black "Super Gremlin" — viral reaction video
  **2021-12-03**, **US urban contemporary radio impact 2022-01-11**, a ~5½-week gap. — [Wikipedia via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Gremlin)
- **Platform-before-radio, artist-attested:** Fetty Wap "Trap Queen" — producer Brian "Peoples"
  Garcia: *"We recorded it in March of 2014, and I started hearing it in August [of 2014]
  everywhere. Then Mr. Cee played it on Hot 97."* The SoundCloud numbers "hit six figures within
  weeks of its release — **without initial blog love, radio play, or industry support.**" —
  [Complex, The Making of "Trap Queen"](https://www.complex.com/music/a/lauren-nostro/the-making-of-fetty-wap-trap-queen); [Billboard 10-year essay](https://www.billboard.com/music/rb-hip-hop/fetty-wap-trap-queen-10-year-anniversary-essay-1235662784/)
- **Platform-before-chart, self-released:** "Whoopty" was **self-released 2020-07-30 and only
  re-released by Warner after CJ signed** on the strength of the virality. — [Wikipedia via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whoopty)
- **Platform-before-chart, independent:** "Ooouuu" was **released independently 2016-05-12**; the
  150,000 radio spins are reported as a downstream consequence of the YouTube views, not a cause. —
  [Wikipedia via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ooouuu); [Forbes, 2016-10-27](https://www.forbes.com/sites/garysuarez/2016/10/27/young-ma-ooouuu/)
- **Platform-before-single:** "The Box" was **never released as a single and had no launch video** —
  TikTok promoted an album cut past Justin Bieber's fully-resourced "Yummy" campaign to No. 1. —
  [Slate, 2020-01](https://slate.com/culture/2020/01/roddy-ricch-the-box-hot-100-tiktok-justin-bieber.html)
- **Platform-before-single, again:** "U Guessed It" — the **video came out 2014-08-28, the single
  2014-09-25.** The meme was released before the product. — [Wikipedia via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U_Guessed_It)
- **Counter-example (label from day one):** "Watch Me (Whip/Nae Nae)" was **released by Capitol
  Records on 2015-05-05** and had a **Nickelodeon promotional partnership running Aug 28–Sep 6,
  2015.** — [Wikipedia via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watch_Me_(Whip/Nae_Nae)); [Songfacts](https://www.songfacts.com/facts/silento/watch-me-whip-nae-nae)
- **Counter-example (not a rap-community record at any stage):** "Old Town Road" ignited among TikTok
  teens doing cowboy transitions, and its next major beat was the Billboard country-chart removal
  controversy — a mainstream-news event, not a hip-hop-community event. — [Capital FM](https://www.capitalfm.com/internet/i-got-the-horses-in-the-back-memes/); [Official Charts](https://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/who-is-lil-nas-x-the-story-behind-the-rappers-viral-smash-old-town-road__26014/)

### Inferences

- The strongest single test for "community first" is **whether the label acted before or after the
  virality**. Signed-after cases (Fetty Wap/300, CJ/Warner, Bobby Shmurda/Epic, Desiigner/GOOD,
  Makonnen/OVO) are unambiguous. Signed-before cases (Silentó/Capitol, Saweetie, NLE Choppa's "Walk
  Em Down", Cardi's "Up") should default to the pop-crossover flag.
- **"In My Feelings" is the hardest borderline case and deserves an explicit synthesizer decision.**
  The creator was a Black Instagram comedian and the first two weeks were community; the artist was
  the most radio-dominant act on the planet. I judge it **community-first in mechanism,
  pop-first in audience** — and would exclude it on the "did an unknown go famous overnight"
  criterion, which it fails completely (Shiggy did; Drake did not).

### Gaps

- I could not obtain **radio add dates / airplay-chart entry dates** for most songs, which is the
  cleanest possible way to time-stamp the platform-vs-radio question. Only "Super Gremlin" yielded
  a dated radio impact.

## Key Question 3: Did an unknown go famous overnight? Label signing within weeks?

### Takeaway

Yes, repeatedly, and the signing lag is often measurable in days — this is the most consistently
documentable proxy for ignition velocity available in this lane.

### Cited Findings

- **iLoveMakonnen:** Drake remix tweet **2014-08-12** → **signed to OVO Sound 2014-08-31**. **19
  days.** — [Wikipedia via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ILoveMakonnen)
- **Desiigner:** "Panda" went "lightly viral" as a street record → Kanye built *TLOP*'s "Father
  Stretch My Hands Pt. 2" on it (album out 2016-02-14) → **signed to GOOD Music/Def Jam shortly
  after** → **No. 1 Hot 100 on 2016-05-07.** — [Stereogum](https://stereogum.com/2280985/the-number-ones-desiigners-panda/columns/the-number-ones); [Wikipedia via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panda_(song))
- **Fetty Wap:** SoundCloud March 2014 → six-figure plays "within weeks" with no industry support →
  **300 Entertainment deal by August 2014.** — [Complex](https://www.complex.com/music/a/lauren-nostro/the-making-of-fetty-wap-trap-queen)
- **CJ:** self-released 2020-07-30 → **Warner deal**, then re-release, then **No. 10 Hot 100 by
  Feb 2021.** — [Wikipedia via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whoopty)
- **Zay Hilfigerrr (15) & Zayion McCall (17):** **18,000 views on 2016-07-08 → 45M on the 2016-08-11
  re-upload → 100M "in like two months" → No. 5 Hot 100.** — [Wikipedia via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juju_on_That_Beat_(TZ_Anthem)); [WXYZ](https://www.wxyz.com/news/detroit-teens-behind-hit-dance-craze-juju-on-the-beat)
- **T-Wayne:** total unknown → **785,562 US copies sold by July 2015**, ~2 months after the
  2015-05-04 release → **No. 9 Hot 100.** — [Wikipedia via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasty_Freestyle)
- **BlocBoy JB:** "Shoot" dance summer 2017 → **"Look Alive" with Drake 2018-02-08 → No. 6 Hot 100
  debut.** ~6–8 months from regional dance to top-10 debut. — [Billboard, 2018-02](https://www.billboard.com/music/rb-hip-hop/blocboy-jb-drake-look-alive-video-shoot-dance-8098902/)
- **GloRilla:** "F.N.F." released **2022-04-29** → **Rolling Loud Miami, Wireless UK, Made in America
  2022** within the same year. — [Wikipedia via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F.N.F._(Let's_Go))
- **Ice Spice:** released **2022-08-10** → **Drake played it on OVO Sound Radio, flew her to Toronto
  and posted her on his IG story in September 2022.** ~1 month. — [Neon Music](https://neonmusic.co.uk/how-ice-spices-munch-feelin-u-lyrics-became-a-tiktok-sensation-and-a-drake-co-sign)
- **Silentó:** "became an overnight sensation at the age of 17." — [The Root](https://www.theroot.com/watch-me-whip-nae-nae-rapper-silent-sentenced-to-2000045173)
- **Counter-case — the creator, not the artist, was the unknown:** Keara Wilson (Savage) and Shiggy
  (In My Feelings) both went from civilian to famous; the artists were already stars. Wilson's clip:
  **15.7M views / 2.4M likes by 2020-03-20.** — [Megan Thee Stallion Wiki](https://megantheestallion.fandom.com/wiki/Savage)

### Inferences

- **Signing lag is the best available substitute for the missing first-2-week velocity data**,
  especially for the Vine era. A label moving in 19 days (Makonnen) or by the next quarter (Fetty,
  CJ, Desiigner) is a labels-panicking signal that no loop count could improve on.
- The 2020s versions of this pipeline are **faster at the top and slower at the bottom**: Ice Spice
  got the Drake co-sign in a month, but artists like Ken Carson accumulate audience over years with
  no ignition event at all. TikTok both accelerated the hits and created a large class of
  community-huge, moment-less artists.

### Gaps

- **Exact contract dates** are rarely public; most of the above are press-reported approximations.
- **Bobby Shmurda's Epic signing date** relative to the Vine spread — I could not pin it.

  2018), and "'Bust down Thotiana' [became] a widespread meme format" on Twitter. — [Blackamericaweb, 2019-02-15](https://blackamericaweb.com/2019/02/15/things-your-kids-know-who-is-blueface-and-what-is-a-thotiana/)

  Yellow' lyrics into what became the internet's funniest meme," including a widely shared

- The "Bad and Boujee" 100 → 600,000 curve is worth foregrounding in the final report **as the one

  of the undocumented cases and is a more honest model than "it exploded on release day."
